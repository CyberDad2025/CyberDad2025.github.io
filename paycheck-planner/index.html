Why the month looks fine and one week does not

Your monthly numbers look fine. You make enough. So why is there a week every month where you are watching the bank app and hoping nothing else comes out?

Because bills do not care about monthly totals. They care about dates. When rent, the car payment and insurance all land before your second paycheck, the first check comes up short, even in a month where you have money left over.

Most budgets show the month. The Paycheck Planner shows each paycheck.

What it does for you

  • See your next paycheck's job before it hits. The Dashboard names the bills it has to pay and the amount left after.
  • Catch a short check weeks ahead. A red SHORT flag and a heads-up line tell you which check will not stretch, so you can move a due date or set money aside while there is still time.
  • Ask "Can I afford it?" and get a straight answer. Type the price and the date. You get YES, YES using your cushion, NOT YET with the first paycheck where it fits without putting a later bill at risk, or NOT IN THIS PLAN.
  • Stop doing paycheck math in your head. Every-2-weeks, weekly, twice-a-month and monthly pay are built in. Bills due on the 31st land correctly in short months.
  • Plan the whole year in one place. 52 paychecks ahead, with a running balance after each one.

FAQ

Do I need Excel?
No. Use the Google Sheets copy link (free Google account). The .xlsx should open in Excel 2016 or later. Both are included.
Does it connect to my bank?
No. Nothing is linked and no login is shared. You type your numbers, so they stay private.
My pay changes every check. Will it work?
Yes. Type the usual amount, then type the real amount on each check when you know it. Everything after updates.
What if I get paid on a different day sometimes?
Type the actual payday on that row. The plan moves the bills to match.
